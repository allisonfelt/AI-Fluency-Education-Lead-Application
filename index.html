01 · The program

I built the thing and then measured it

At Apple Education, capable people kept giving inconsistent answers about products they had sold for years. Nobody had built them a way to understand what they were explaining.

So I built one. Elevate is a four-week program I proposed, designed, and shipped — curriculum, sequencing, and the call on what to leave out. It ran against one question: could people explain the product correctly a month later, without me in the room?

They could. 24% average knowledge lift. It won the organization's 2025 Impact Award from 500+ candidates, but the measurement is the part that matters. You wrote that completion is not comprehension. I instrumented for that before I had to defend it.

03 · The pipeline

The relevant part is throughput. Fifteen to twenty finished pieces a month. I tripled video output by changing how we sourced subjects, not by hiring. Open rates rose 80% once I looked at the audience data. And I started a faculty series nobody asked for, because we were missing a segment.
